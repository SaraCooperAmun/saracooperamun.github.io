### Overview

During my MSc in Robotics at Heriot-Watt University (2018-2019) I investigated how humans plan and execute **physical handover tasks with robots**, combining human–robot interaction (HRI) and cognitive neuroscience methods.

The MSc thesis results were presented at ICHMS 2020 and won the best Paper Award:
["An EEG investigation on planning human-robot handover tasks"](/publication/2020-09-handover)

The study explored how the human brain adapts when interacting with a robot compared to another human during a controlled object handover task.

### User-case / experimental setup

Participants were asked to perform object handovers in two conditions:
- human–human handover  
- human–robot handover (using a TIAGo robot)

In each trial:
- participants prepared to grasp and pass an object
- gaze behavior was recorded
- EEG was measured to capture motor planning activity
- interaction timing and coordination were analyzed

The robot acted as the receiver in structured handover scenarios, enabling controlled comparison between human–human and human–robot joint action.

---

### Key findings

The results showed that:

- participants reported **positive interaction experience** with the robot  
- gaze patterns during robot handovers resembled those used in human–human interaction  
- EEG signals indicated **increased motor readiness during human–robot handovers**  
- this suggests that interacting with robots introduces additional **planning and cognitive load** compared to human partners  

The findings highlight that humans do not treat robots as passive objects; instead, they engage in adapted motor planning strategies when coordinating actions with them.

This supports the idea that:
- gaze acts as a key non-verbal coordination cue in HRI  
- human–robot joint action engages similar—but not identical—neural mechanisms to human–human interaction  
- robot embodiment influences cognitive planning during collaborative physical tasks
