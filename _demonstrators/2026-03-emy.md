### Overview
At **HRI 2026**, I showcased **Emy**, a socially assistive robot designed to support therapeutic activities with children on the autism spectrum. The demonstration highlighted how Emy’s expressive capabilities — including facial expressions, head and body motion, LEDs, and expressive sounds — can facilitate **engagement and interactive learning** as part of autism therapy. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  

Visitors could experience:

- **Expressive engagement:** Emy uses expressive movements, gaze, LEDs, and sounds to attract and sustain children’s attention.  
- **Vocabulary games:** Interactive games designed to teach categories such as **emotions, animals, food, and colors** in a playful, child‑friendly format.  
- **Adaptive interaction:** Emy’s social responses help make interaction predictable and engaging for children with autism, supporting therapeutic goals through playful learning.

More about the paper can be found on the ACM Digital Library: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3776734.3794581.

### My Contribution
I contributed to:

- **Robot behaviour and interaction design** focusing on expressive cues tailored for engagement with children on the autism spectrum.  
- **Design and deployment of interactive vocabulary learning games** using Emy to support early learning concepts.  
- **Running live demonstrations** at the HRI conference and engaging attendees in discussions of therapeutic applications.  
- Highlighting how expressive socially assistive robots can enhance **therapeutic engagement and early cognitive development** in ASD populations.

This demonstration was a rewarding opportunity to connect my research on socially assistive robotics with clinicians, researchers, and educators interested in autism therapy applications.
