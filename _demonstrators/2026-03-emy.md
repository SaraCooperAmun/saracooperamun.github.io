### Overview
At **HRI 2026**, I showcased **Emy**, a socially assistive robot designed to support therapeutic activities with children on the autism spectrum. The robot has been developed as part of EMOROBCARE project between IIIA-CSIC (Barcelona) and ICMAT-CSIC (Madrid), co-designed together with therapists and tested in about 22 children.

The demonstration highlighted how Emy’s expressive capabilities — including facial expressions, head and body motion, LEDs, and expressive sounds — can facilitate **engagement and interactive learning** as part of autism therapy. 

Visitors could experience:

---

- **Idle behavior & sensor-driven interaction:**  
  Emy reacts when touched on different body areas (face and body sensors) and responds to motion events such as shaking or movement using its accelerometer. These behaviors allow the robot to “wake up” and naturally respond to physical interaction.

- **Interaction manager & expressive system:**  
  The interaction manager orchestrates Emy’s multimodal expressive system, combining motion, facial expressions, LEDs, speech prosody, TTS, and audio to generate coherent affective states such as happiness, sadness, surprise, and skepticism.

  This system is further described in our **ICRA 2026 workshop paper**, presented in Vienna:

- **Vocabulary games:** Interactive games designed to teach categories such as **emotions, animals, food, and colors** in a playful, child-friendly format.

  One of the main activities demonstrated was the **Emotions Game**, where the robot teaches children how to recognize and correctly name different facial expressions such as happiness, sadness, anger, and surprise.
