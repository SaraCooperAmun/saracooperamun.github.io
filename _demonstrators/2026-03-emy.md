### Overview

The EMOROBCARE project is a multidisciplinary research initiative between IIIA-CSIC (Barcelona) and ICMAT-CSIC (Madrid) focused on developing a low-cost, expressive social robot to support therapy for children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). The project is grounded in a human-centered approach to social AI, combining robotics, artificial intelligence, and cognitive science to design assistive technologies that can be meaningfully integrated into real therapeutic practices.

More information about the project can be found here:  
👉 https://socialminds.iiia.csic.es/projects/emorobcare/

The work is developed within the SocialMinds Lab at IIIA-CSIC, which provides an interdisciplinary environment combining social robotics, AI for healthcare and education, and experimental human–robot interaction research infrastructures. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}  

EMOROBCARE emphasizes:
- affordability and open-source development  
- co-design with therapists and domain experts  
- deployment in real therapeutic environments  
- support for structured interaction, communication, and emotional development in children with ASD :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  

Within this project, Emy is a small tabletop robot designed to act as a socially assistive companion during therapy sessions. It supports a triadic interaction model between therapist, child, and robot, where the robot provides structured, engaging, and predictable interaction while the therapist guides the session. The system has been co-designed with therapists and evaluated with around 22 children.

This work was carried out during my research at IIIA-CSIC (April 2025 – June 2026), focusing on social interaction design and system integration for human–robot interaction in therapeutic settings.

### System Description

Emy is a compact (~30 cm) social robot with a spherical base and a 3-DOF head enabling expressive motion (pan, tilt, roll). It includes a camera, microphone, speakers, and a screen-based face used to display animated expressions. The system is built around a ROS 2 architecture running on embedded hardware (Jetson Nano), combined with a tablet interface for interactive content.

The software architecture integrates perception, reasoning, communication, and behaviour coordination modules:

- **Perception**
  - Face and body detection, recognition, and engagement tracking  
  - Object detection using YOLO-based models and open-vocabulary approaches  
  - Pointing gesture recognition to support deictic interaction  
  - Depth estimation for spatial reasoning  

- **Communication**
  - Speech recognition using Whisper and Vosk  
  - Expressive text-to-speech (XTTS) with emotional prosody  
  - LLM-based dialogue adapted to therapeutic contexts  
  - Multimodal output combining speech, motion, and facial expressions  

- **Behaviour and interaction**
  - Intent-based controller coordinating games and interaction flow  
  - Knowledge representation of objects, users, and context  
  - Attention manager for gaze and engagement  
  - Expressive behaviours combining motion, animation, and sound  

- **Interactive system**
  - Web-based interface for therapist control and games  
  - Real-time visualization tools  
  - Integration of speech, touch, and pointing input  

The system enables structured therapeutic activities such as vocabulary learning, joint attention, and emotion recognition. In interactive tasks, the robot combines perception, dialogue, and expressive feedback to guide the child, interpret responses, and provide adaptive support.
