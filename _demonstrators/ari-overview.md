## Introduction

During my work as a robotics software engineer and technical expert for ARI at PAL Robotics (2020–2023), I contributed to the development, integration, and deployment of the ARI social robot across multiple healthcare and assistive robotics projects.

My responsibilities included supporting product management, defining technical requirements, and acting as technical contact for EU and national projects such as AMIBA, SPRING, and SHAPES. I also participated in demonstrations at conferences and industry events, showcasing ARI’s capabilities in real-world scenarios.

---

## ARI Robot

ARI is a social robotics platform developed by PAL Robotics for human–robot interaction in healthcare, assistive living, and research contexts.

Key characteristics include:

- Mobile autonomous base for indoor navigation  
- Interactive head and display for social communication  
- Multimodal interaction (speech, touch, visual feedback)  
- Integration with external systems via ROS-based architectures  
- Designed for long-term deployment in environments such as hospitals and care facilities  

ARI was used as the main robotic platform in several European research projects, including SHAPES.

Related publication:  
["ARI: the Social Assistive Robot and Companion"](/publication/2020-08-ari)

["Challenges of deploying assistive robots in real-life scenarios: an industrial perspective"](/publication/2023-08-challenges)

Asides EU projects, I also worked on ROS4HRI and the dialogue pipeline of the robot, see more details here ["Open-source natural language processing on the pal robotics ari social robot"](/publication/2023-03-nlp-ari)

## AMIBA Project

ARI was deployed at the AMIBA Foundation as part of a pilot focused on evaluating social robotics in care and rehabilitation environments. The AMIBA Foundation is a non-profit organisation dedicated to supporting elderly people and individuals with disabilities through day-care services, rehabilitation, and social inclusion programs.

Further details about the project can be found here:  
[ARI at AMIBA Foundation](https://pal-robotics.com/blog/social-robot-ari-as-a-robot-assistant-at-amiba-foundation/)

![ARI at AMIBA Foundation](/images/amiba.png)

In this project, ARI was explored as a robotic assistant to support staff and users in daily activities such as:

- Greeting users and supporting daily routines  
- Encouraging participation in group and therapeutic activities  
- Delivering reminders and announcements in care environments  
- Supporting physical and cognitive stimulation exercises  

My contribution included both technical development and user-centered design work. I worked on the development of the application deployed on ARI and supported the implementation of interaction behaviours for the pilot. I also contributed to the co-design process, including on-site visits, engagement with caregivers and users, and iterative refinement of the robot’s behaviour based on feedback from real-world deployments.

Related publication:  
["Towards the deployment of a social robot at an elderly day care facility"](/publication/2022-12-amiba)

## SPRING Project

The SPRING (Socially Pertinent Robot Interaction for a Governance Network) project was a European H2020 initiative focused on deploying social robots in public service environments to support interaction, guidance, and information delivery to citizens.

Project website:  
[https://spring-h2020.eu/](https://spring-h2020.eu/)

---

### Use Case: Receptionist and Public Information Assistant

In SPRING, ARI was deployed as a receptionist-style robot in public-facing environments such as service centres and public institutions. The goal was to evaluate how a social robot can act as a first point of contact for users.

The robot was designed to support:

- Welcoming and guiding visitors in public spaces  
- Providing information about services, locations, and procedures  
- Assisting with simple interaction flows (e.g., FAQs, directions, and navigation support)  
- Reducing workload for human staff by handling repetitive reception tasks  
- Acting as an accessible and engaging interface for citizens  

The focus was on robust long-term interaction in semi-structured, dynamic environments where users have diverse needs and behaviours.

My work in SPRING focused on software integration and system-level development of ARI within the project architecture in the following deliverable [SPRING D7.3 – Initial Software Architecture for SPRING-ARI](https://spring-h2020.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/SPRING_D7.3_Initial_Software_Architecture_For_SPRING-ARI_VFinal_Amended_29_06_2022.pdf)

Key contributions included:

- Integration of ARI software components into the SPRING system architecture  
- Development and adaptation of robot behaviours for receptionist-style interaction  
- Software engineering of perception-to-action pipelines using ROS-based frameworks  
- Integration of external modules for dialogue, interaction management, and service logic  
- Ensuring system modularity and robustness for deployment in real-world public environments  

A major part of the integration relied on **ROS4HRI**.

ROS4HRI (Robot Operating System for Human-Robot Interaction) is a standardized framework for representing and managing human-related information in ROS-based systems.

It defines common conventions for:
- Human representation (person identity, face, body, voice tracking)  
- Standardized ROS topics and namespaces for HRI data  
- Modular perception pipelines (e.g., detection, tracking, recognition)  
- Interoperable interfaces for HRI components across systems  

This allows different perception modules and interaction components to work together in a consistent and scalable way.

More information:  
[ROS4HRI framework](https://github.com/ros4hri)

In SPRING, ROS4HRI was used as the backbone for human perception and interaction data flow, enabling consistent handling of users in public environments and simplifying integration between perception, dialogue, and behaviour systems.
